# UI, audio, look, effects, foliage and animation

Read this when you touch screens and menus, sound and music, mesh looks, particle effects and decals, lights and colour grading, scatter, or animsets and baked motion. Part of the [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) index.

## Listening to the game

- Sound is resolved on the simulation clock and is the same without a window: listen to the game through `audio` and the `MUSIC` line in `observe`, not through
  the speakers (except the sound of the `AnimationCue` clip marker: it exists only if a model is drawn). Music changes go
  to the journal as lines like `[local] music: arena explore → combat` (they are not game events). The sandbox's and the template's sound files are synthesised by `cargo run -p sandbox --example make_sounds`.

## UI

- **The HUD and menus are content** (`*.screen.ron`, the theme is `*.theme.ron`), not window code: the game gives widgets their values with
  `add_ui_source`, and button actions with `add_ui_action`. Screens are outside the simulation (no `AiId`, not in the snapshot); anything that changes the world,
  a button does through a tool (`tool …`) or a game action, and the replay repeats the press itself (`ui_click`, `ui_navigate`).

## Sound

- **Sound is content** (`*.sound.ron`, `*.music.ron`, the mixer `*.audio.ron`): an attack names its sound in `telegraph.sound`, and an action
  plays the effect `Sound("whoosh")` at its own moment (the swing); the game plays its own `PlaySound`, and a music layer waits for a condition by name
  (`add_ai_condition`). Sound only reads the world and does not change it; what only this machine shows is written to the journal as
  `JournalKind::Local`, not `Event` (the host sends events to clients). Sound is resolved on the simulation clock and is the same without a window and on
  a co-op client, so a sound the game must always hear is `telegraph.sound` or the `Sound` effect, not a clip marker:
  its sound (`AnimationCue` with the sound's name) exists only where a model is drawn, and `audio` without a window will not show it.
- **Placed sounds are emitters** (F10): a sound with `looped: true` plays only from a `SoundEmitter { sound, playing }` (a component, so a level node, a prefab or code places it; not `AiId` state, `replay_ignore`d, its journal lines `Local`), never as a one-shot (a `Sound` effect, a telegraph or a mixer event that names one is a content error). The mixer holds one voice per emitter while the listener is within `far`, under the normal priority rules (a one-shot of a lower priority never takes a loop's voice); `refresh` runs in both `FixedPostUpdate` and `Update`, so emitter code must start nothing when nothing changed. Walls are opt-in (`occlusion: Some((gain: 0.3))` in the mixer): a `WORLD` ray from the listener through `PhysicsWorld`, a collider's `SoundTransmission` (else the mixer's gain) per collider, multiplied; a gain only, since Bevy's sinks have no per-voice filter. Order emitters by name and place, never by `Entity`.

## Looks

- **Mesh looks are content** (`*.look.ron`): the look sets the colour, the light in bands, the flat faces, the outline and the glow; a prefab picks its look with
  the component `"Look": ("hero")`, and the code gives a mesh one white material and `Look::new("stone")` on the mesh itself. Only the
  picture changes (the `render` feature, a window or `--offscreen`), never the gameplay. A `Look` in a body's prefab is an ordinary component (replay,
  snapshot, co-op clients), so a `Look` that only the picture needs is put by the code on a child mesh without `AiId`, not on the
  body; a shared style is a preset (`extends: "low_poly"`); the colour and the outline thickness do not go into game code.

## Foliage

- **Foliage is content** (`*.scatter.ron`): the definition decides what grows where, how densely and in which kinds, and
  the layout comes from the region's seed, the cell and the id through `DetRng` (never `SimRng`). Instances are for the picture only: without `AiId` and
  colliders, under the tile's root; whatever must block walking or mean something in the game is placed in the tile as a prefab or a collider.
  Foliage kinds have no outline (a kind with an outline is a content error: each instance would get its own shell). The ground and
  obstacles for the layout come only from static colliders (`Body::Static`): a platform that moves does not hold the grass.

## Effects

- **Effects are content** (`*.effect.ron`, the event table is one `*.fx.ron`): the effect sets the number of particles, their life, speed, colours and pool, and
  a combat event (`damaged`, `died`, …) or a clip marker picks it from the table, and the game plays it at its own moment with
  `PlayEffect`. Effects and decals are only pictures (the `render` feature, a window or `--offscreen`): they do not change the world, their entities have no
  `AiId`, and the particle clock is the simulation clock (pause freezes it). Effect numbers are not put in game code.
- **A play carries its strength and its push** (`PlayOptions`, `PlayEffect::strength` and `::push`, a cue's `strength`): how an effect answers them (more, faster or bigger particles; the share of the push each takes) is its own content (`strength`, `push`), and its look over a life (`size_ease`, `swell`, `Disc`, `unlit`) too. A play without a push draws no number, so existing effects start the particles they always did; keep new fields off by default.
- **Gore is content too (F9): marks and debris that lasts.** A spot on a surface is a `mark` (`*.mark.ron`, not simulation content): a decal of Bevy's standard forward-decal material (lit), its quad turned so that its +Y is the surface's normal, a pool per kind with a cap that recycles the oldest laid (`MarkPool`, by its `seq`, never by time, since marks of one tick share it); the game lays one with `spawn_mark` or `PlayMark`, and the `fx` table's `marks` lays them from `shot_impact`, `shot_hit`, `damaged`, `died` and `part_broken` (`MarkLink`: `place: At | Behind(m) | Below(m)`, `count`, `spread`, `offset`). A shot fires both `shot_hit` and `damaged`: link them to different marks. Randomness is the picture's: a seed from the event (`mark_seed`: its point, its normal, the pellet), never `SimRng`, so a client lays what the host does. Debris is an effect with `collide: true`: its particles are stepped on the simulation's clock with a fixed 1/60 s (`debris::Debris`, pure and tested without Bevy against a `Ground` trait), a ray a step through `PhysicsWorld::raycast` on `WORLD` (read-only, so no line in `readers.allow`), at most `GoreStats::budget` rays a frame; `rest: UntilCleared` keeps a particle that has come to rest past the 30 s cap (`Particle::alive`: at rest for good, or not yet stepped to its end), and the pool's `max_live` is the cap. Only the picture's: no `AiId`, no state a replay or a snapshot sees (`WorldRebuilt` and leaving `InGame` clear it: `shards_fx::clear`, `clear_all`, the tool `fx_clear`), nothing on a machine that does not draw (`draws(world)` in every observer). A `mesh` particle may have no UVs: the particle shader guards `in.uv` with `VERTEX_UVS_A`, and a new shader line that reads a vertex attribute must do the same. `PartBroken` is not a net event: chunks linked to `part_broken` show on the host and in solo play. Detail: `docs/manual.md`, "Gore: marks and debris (F9)".
- **Light past white is content too.** An effect's `intensity` multiplies its light; only a camera that draws in HDR keeps light above white, and the camera's picture (`picture.bloom`, `picture.tone_mapping` in the `camera` content) is what turns HDR and a bloom on. Do not add `Bloom`, `Hdr` or `Tonemapping` to a camera in game code for the look: put it in the content, which leaves a camera as the game spawned it while it says nothing. Under HDR, particles are tone mapped like everything else (their shader has no tone mapping of its own).

## Lights and colour grading

- **Lights are content** (`*.light.ron`, kind `light`, `shards_light`): kind (`Point`, or `Spot(inner_deg, outer_deg)` with the cone's whole opening in degrees, along the node's -Z), `color` (`#rrggbb`) or `kelvin`, `intensity` in Bevy's lumens, `range`, `radius`, `shadows`, `flicker`. A `ContentLight { id }` on an entity places one (a level node's `light=<id>`, a prefab's `"ContentLight": (id: "lamp")`, code). Picture only (`SIMULATION = false`): with `render` the module puts a Bevy `PointLight`/`SpotLight` on a **child** entity (`LightPicture`, no `AiId`; `ContentLight` itself requires `Transform` and, with `render`, `Visibility`, so the entity that carries it has the same components headless and drawn), headless it only keeps `LightState`. Do not put a Bevy light on a gameplay entity for the look, and do not read a light in a tick. A game's own Bevy lights (a sun, a glTF lamp with no `light=`) are the game's: the module never touches them.
- **Flicker is a pure function of the simulation's clock** (`Time<Fixed>` elapsed plus overstep, `shards_motion::camera::picture_clock`): a pause freezes it and `step` moves it, a headless game and a drawn one agree, and a test at a known tick knows the letter. A noise flicker is seeded by the light's content id, node name and place as they were when it first appeared (`flicker::seed_of`, kept in `LightSeeds` while the entity is placed, so a carried or moved light keeps its wander and `accessibility.flashes` still caps its rate), never `SimRng`; the position-based `key` is for ordering and ties only. A new per-light random value is taken once per entity the same way. The player's `accessibility.flashes` shapes it (`flicker::shaped`, `level_for`): reduced is no faster than 2 a second and half as far from the light as written, off is steady, and the `lights` query shows the flicker as drawn. New flicker kinds go in `flicker::level` as pure functions of `(clock, seed)`.
- **The shadow budget** (`graphics.shadow_lights`, `GraphicsSettings::shadow_light_budget`) is `budget::casters`, a pure function of the lights' distances to the viewer (the `AiCamera`, else a `ThirdPersonCamera` or `FirstPersonCamera`, else the local player's eye; with none of them there is no viewer: `lights` says so, every distance is unknown and ties go by the key alone), ties by a stable key (definition id, name, place), never by `Entity`. Only content lights that ask (`shadows: true`) take part.
- **Colour grading is the camera's picture** (`picture.grading`, `shards_motion::camera::grading`): a `Grading` (exposure, white balance `temperature`/`tint`, `hue_deg`, `saturation`, `contrast`, `lift`/`gamma`/`gain`) becomes Bevy's `ColorGrading` on the world cameras; Bevy grades before tone mapping, so `tone_mapping: None` shows none of it. A `GradingVolume { id }` on a trigger volume (a level node's `grading=<id>` with `marker = trigger`) blends the picture toward the `grading` definition while the local player's body touches it (`MotionState::volumes`; the last in collider id order wins), over its `blend_s`, and back to the camera's own. `GradingBlend` is a resource of the picture, tracked in `FixedLast` (exact ticks, `step` included) and applied in `Update`; it is in no snapshot or replay. Add a camera kind the picture reaches to `apply_grading` (the same cameras as `apply_picture`) and copy its `ColorGrading` to any second camera that draws the world.
- **Check it** with the `lights` query (counts, flicker now, who casts a shadow and why not, the grade and its blend), headless (`lights.scenario`) and offscreen (`sandbox/tests/sandbox/lights.rs`: a flashing lamp at two moments, a grading volume's channel means).

## Animation

- **Animation is content** (`*.animset.ron`): an animset sets which clip from which glTF file shows a state or an action, the blending and the markers
  (`cues` → sound and effect; a cue may name a `bone` and a `strength`: it plays at the bone's world place, scaled, and is presentation only, D1.7); a Blender script builds the models and clips (`tools/blender/humanoid.py`, only `-b
  --factory-startup`, without a window), and its output is committed. `Animated` and the model are only a picture: the game puts them on the body when
  `config.draws()`; the replay does not check `Animated`, and the gameplay does not depend on the clip. An action's offset (`displacement`) with `bake:
  (animset, clip)` is baked from the clip's root bone: **if you change the clip, bake it again** (`cargo sandbox --bake-motion`, tool
  `motion_bake`); otherwise `--check-content` names the stale curve. Do not edit such a curve by hand. A new bake changes
  the gameplay: the reference replays then diverge and are re-recorded.
- **Sockets, props and clip families are presentation too** (`animset`: `sockets`, `carry`, `mounts`, `by_moveset`; the kind `prop`, `*.prop.ron`; `shards_action::render::props`). The engine spawns the props under the socket's bone, without `AiId`, so they never reach a replay, a snapshot or a client; where a prop sits is read every frame from the action's time and the seconds since a weapon switch (`mount_places`, pure), never from a clip's cue. Do not give a prop an `AiId`, do not read a bone or a prop in `FixedUpdate`, and do not add a presentation component to a body that the replay or the snapshot would carry: follow `Animated` and `PropSwaps` (reflected, `replay_ignore`, `net_ignore`, inserted by the game only where something is drawn). Every new `animset` field serialises only when set (`skip_serializing_if`), because the content hash feeds the co-op handshake: a test per file (`existing_animsets_keep_their_json`) pins the JSON of the sets a change leaves alone. A walk of the skeleton by bone name goes through `render::props::skeleton`, which skips the subtrees below a `PropOf`; a prop's glTF gets no `AnimationPlayer` (`retarget_on_ready` returns below a `PropOf`). A change to `sockets`, `carry` or `mounts` rebuilds nothing (only `clips`, `layers`, layered mappings and `by_moveset` shape the graph); the props go over to a new binding. A game's own attachment (the core crystal on the chest) reads `Sockets`, it does not spawn a prop of its own. The tool `animation id=eN [animset=humanoid]` says what the bridge shows or would show (headless too); the counts are in `ai/status` -> `anim`.
- **Motion modes and the model's own writes (D1.2).** `mounts["@mode:<mode>"]` and `mounts["@left:<mode>"]` are two more clocks of the same pure `mount_places` (the order: cut hold, running action, mode, left, equip, carry socket); their per-body state is `render::modes::ModeState` (advanced once a frame in `drive` from the drawn mode, whether an action runs and the simulation clock, so a pause freezes it and a hold shifts it; the wait, the linger and the drop are pure and tested without Bevy). The mode a body is drawn in is `MotionState::mode` and nothing else; a game that wants another reason to step aside does it in content. `ModeLeft` is a presentation event: observe it for effects, never for gameplay. Anything the bridge writes on the model itself goes on a **bone** in `PostUpdate`, in the `AnimSystems` stages (`Root`, then `Model`: after `AnimationSystems` and `InterpSystems::Blend`, before `Propagate`), never on the model child that carries `Interpolated` (its blend takes any foreign write as the new rest and compounds it); `apply_model` converts metres into the bone's parent space and takes last frame's write off first when the clips did not rewrite the bone. Lengths in the world (drops, later lunges) are metres, lengths on a bone are the rig's units. A layer's `off_in` is read in place (it is not part of the graph's shape).
- **Event overlays (D1.6) are presentation.** The observers in `render::bridge` (`overlay_events`) only note an event on the drawn body's `AnimationBound`; `drive` starts the overlay on the next frame and ages it on the simulation clock (`Time<Fixed>` elapsed plus overstep), so a pause freezes it and a hit stop shifts it. Never make gameplay wait on an overlay or read one in `FixedUpdate`. A new engine event an overlay should answer goes in both `OVERLAY_EVENTS` (`anim.rs`, the names the check and the tool know) and `overlay_events` (its observer, on the body the event names). An overlay's clip plays on its own masked node (`Built::overlay_node`), so it never fights an action's layered node for the same clip; a change to `overlays` reshapes the graph like a layered mapping does.
- **Render interpolation is presentation** (`shards_motion::interp`): a drawn child of a simulated body carries `Interpolated` and is moved in `PostUpdate`
  (`InterpSystems::Blend`) between the body's last two tick poses. Never write a body's `Transform` for the picture's sake (physics reads it), never put
  interpolation state on an entity with an `AiId`, and put a game system that reads where a body is *drawn* (a camera, a marker) after `InterpSystems::Blend`
  and use `shown_translation`. A visual child that must stay at the tick's pose carries `NotInterpolated`. The `graphics.interpolation` setting off must
  give exactly the old picture.

## First-person view and the view model (F1b)

- **The camera writes `Intent.look` only on a frame the look axis moved**, starts from the look the intent holds and never from a stored yaw (`first_person_look`), so an agent's `look`, a recorded input and a replay that plays (`AiReplay`) own the value; the picture reads `Intent.look` every frame. Never read the camera in a tick: the eye and the ray are `eye_of` and `look_dir`. Maths and numbers stay window-free in `camera/first_person/mod.rs` (the `first_person` section of the `camera` kind: every field `skip_serializing_if` its default, every number checked in `problems()`); systems are behind `render`.
- **Layers are part of the picture's contract**: `OWN_BODY_LAYER` = 62 (the looked-through body's meshes, hidden from the first-person camera, drawn by cameras that have it) and `VIEW_MODEL_LAYER` = 63 (the view model, drawn by its own camera only). A light lights a camera only if they share a layer, and a shadow caster counts only for a light that shares its layer, so lights and other 3D cameras without `RenderLayers` get the layers while a first-person camera exists (`share_lights`, `other_cameras_see_the_body`); a game that gives a light or a camera its own layers adds them. Do not put another meaning on these numbers or on layer 0.
- **A view model is a picture-only rig**: `Animated` plus `ShowsBody(body)` under a second camera (`ViewModelCamera`, one `order` above the world camera, `ClearColorConfig::None`, the definition's own fov). The bridge reads the body's `ActionState`, `MotionState`, `Fighter` and the rest through `ShowsBody` (`drive`'s `Followed` query); never copy gameplay components (`ActionState`, `Fighter`) onto a rig, the action machine would tick it as a second fighter. No `AiId`, nothing in snapshots or replays; its meshes get the view model layer, `NotShadowCaster` and `NoFrustumCulling` as they appear. Which one is `MovesetDef::view_model` (an id: simulation content, left out when `None`) else `camera` content's `first_person.view_model`; the `view_model` kind itself is a picture (`SIMULATION = false`). The UI goes to the highest-`order` camera, so the HUD stays over the arms; an HDR world camera shares its main texture with the view model camera, which then draws untone-mapped.
- **The listener** of a first-person body (it faces its look, or a `FirstPersonCamera` looks through it) is `eye_of(body)`, the simulation's eye, not the camera's: audio is decided on the simulation clock and must agree with and without a window.
